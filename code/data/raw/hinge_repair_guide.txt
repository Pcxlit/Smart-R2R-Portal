Laptop Hinge Repair Guide (Generic 13-15 inch models)

Symptom: The laptop hinge feels loose, makes a cracking sound when opening,
or the screen does not stay upright on its own.

Tools needed: Phillips #0 and #1 screwdrivers, plastic spudger, tweezers.

Step 1: Power off the laptop and disconnect the battery if it is
user-removable. Working on a powered device risks short-circuiting the
motherboard.

Step 2: Remove the bottom panel screws. Note that screws near the hinge
are often a different length than the rest -- keep them separated.

Step 3: Gently pry the bottom panel using the plastic spudger, starting
from a corner away from the hinge to avoid stressing already-weak plastic.

Step 4: Locate the hinge assembly, usually two metal brackets screwed into
the chassis on either side of the display. Loose hinges are usually caused
by stripped screws or a cracked plastic mounting point.

Step 5: If screws are stripped, replace with a slightly wider gauge screw
of the same thread pitch. If the plastic mount is cracked, a hinge
replacement kit (chassis-specific) is required rather than a screw fix.

Step 6: Reassemble in reverse order. Test the hinge through a full range
of motion before reinstalling the bottom panel screws fully.

Common mistake: Over-tightening replacement screws can re-crack a
already-weak plastic mount. Hand-tighten only.
